You open your task list on Monday and the top of it is red. Twelve overdue tasks, some from last week, one from September. Each one is a small reminder that you said you would do something and did not.

Overdue tasks are normal. Every list that has dates gets them. The trouble starts when they pile up, because then the list stops being a plan. You skim past the red part to find today's tasks, and the old ones get older.

This post is about clearing that pile in one short sitting, and about keeping it small afterwards.

## Why tasks go overdue

It helps to know which kind of overdue task you are looking at, because each kind needs a different fix.

Some tasks slipped because the day was too full. You planned seven things for Tuesday and did four. Nothing is wrong with those three tasks. They need a new day.

Some tasks slipped because nobody needs them anymore. The event happened, someone else did it, or you changed your mind. They stay on the list only because deleting them feels like admitting defeat.

Some tasks slipped because they are too big for one sitting. "Sort out taxes" or "Clean the garage" sits there for weeks, because there is no point in the day where you start it. Each time you see it, you move it again.

## Clear the pile in ten minutes

Set a timer for ten minutes and go through the overdue tasks from the oldest to the newest. Give each task one decision and move on. Do not do the tasks yet, except the ones that take less time than deciding about them, like replying to a message.

For each task, pick one of three things.

### Reschedule it to a real day

If you still want to do it, give it the day you will actually do it. Not "tomorrow" by reflex. Look at the coming days and pick one with room. A task moved to tomorrow out of habit is overdue again by Wednesday.

If several tasks belong to the same day, move them together. Most of the time that day is today or the next free day.

### Delete it

If you would not add this task today, delete it. That is the whole test. A task that has been overdue for a month and that nobody has asked about is usually one you can let go.

Deleting a task you will not do makes the list match your real week. A short list you trust is more useful than a long one you skim.

### Split it

If you have moved the same task three times, it is probably too big. Replace it with the first step that you could finish in one go. "Sort out taxes" becomes "Find last year's return" on Wednesday. "Clean the garage" becomes "Take the boxes to the recycling centre" on Saturday.

You do not need to plan every step. Write the first one, give it a day, and delete the big task. When the first step is done, add the next one.

## Overdue tasks in Soonlane

Soonlane puts every overdue task in one place. The Overdue section sits at the top of Upcoming and of Today, above today's tasks. Each overdue task shows the date it was due in red, so you can tell the one from yesterday apart from the one from September. A task stays there until you complete it, move it or delete it.

Here is how the three decisions look in the app.

To reschedule everything at once, use the Reschedule button on the right of the Overdue heading. It opens the date picker. Pick Today, Tomorrow, Next week or another quick date, click a day in the calendar, or type one, such as `fri` or `14 Nov`. Every task in that Overdue section moves to that day. Tasks keep their due times, so the 10:30 call is still at 10:30.

On a label's page, the Overdue section has its own Reschedule button, and it moves only the overdue tasks with that label. That is handy when the pile is mostly one kind of thing, like errands.

To reschedule one task, open the "…" menu on its row. The Date row there has Today, Tomorrow, Next week and, from Monday to Thursday, This weekend. "More dates" opens the full date picker.

To delete a task, choose Delete in the same "…" menu. Soonlane asks you to confirm first, because a deleted task is gone for good.

To split a task, add the first step under the day you will do it, then delete the original. Every day in Upcoming has its own "Add task" row for this.

If you just want the red out of sight while you work on today, the Overdue heading folds. Click it to collapse the section and again to open it. Collapsing only hides the list. The tasks are still overdue.

On the Mac app, the Dock icon counts tasks that are overdue or due today. If that number keeps growing, it is time for the ten-minute pass.

## Keep the pile small

Clearing overdue tasks once is easy. Keeping the section short takes two habits.

Plan fewer tasks per day than you think you can do. If you usually finish four, plan four, not seven. The extra three end up overdue anyway, and now they come with guilt. [Planning your week in a day-by-day list](/blog/plan-your-week-with-an-upcoming-view) shows a short routine that spreads tasks over the week before it starts.

Look at Overdue every morning, not once a week. One or two tasks a day are a 30-second decision. Twelve on a Monday are a chore you put off, which is how the September task got there. Soonlane's Today view shows only the Overdue section and today's tasks, so it is a good place to start the day.

## What about repeating tasks?

A repeating task can be overdue too. If you set "Water the plants" for every day and miss three days, you do not need three catch-up waterings. In Soonlane, completing an overdue repeating task moves it to the first date in its schedule that is today or later, so the missed days do not pile up. [Repeating tasks](/blog/repeating-tasks) explains how that next date is picked.

When Reschedule moves a repeating task, it changes only that occurrence's date. The task keeps its rule, so a task that repeats every Monday still comes back on Mondays after you complete it.
