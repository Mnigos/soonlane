Todoist's free plan is called Beginner. It is a real plan, not a trial, and many people never leave it. But it has limits, and some of them only show up after a few weeks of use, when you try to add a sixth project or a fourth filter.

Below are the limits that matter day to day, what each one means in practice, and how to tell whether the free plan is enough for you. The numbers come from Todoist's pricing page and its "Usage limits" help article, as of November 2026. Todoist changes its plans from time to time, so check those pages before you decide.

## The limits at a glance

|                                          | Beginner (free)     | Pro                                |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Price                                    | $0                  | $5/month billed yearly, $7 monthly |
| Active personal projects                 | 5                   | 300                                |
| Filters                                  | 3                   | 150                                |
| Reminders                                | Automatic only      | Automatic and custom               |
| Activity history                         | 1 week              | Full                               |
| File uploads                             | 5 MB                | 100 MB                             |
| Layouts                                  | List and Board      | List, Board, Calendar and Table    |
| Task duration and deadlines              | No                  | Yes                                |
| AI capture (Ramble, Task Capture Assist) | 10 sessions a month | Unlimited, fair use                |
| Calendar accounts connected              | 1                   | Up to 3                            |
| Automatic backups                        | No                  | Yes                                |

Some limits are the same on every plan, so paying does not raise them. You get up to 500 labels per account, 300 active tasks per project, 20 sections per project and 5 people per personal project.

## Five projects

This is the limit most people hit first. Sub-projects count towards the 5, so a "Work" project with three sub-projects uses 4 of your 5.

If you start on Pro and later drop back to the free plan with more than 5 projects, Todoist keeps them, but the extra ones become view-only. You can read them, but you cannot change them.

The way around it is labels. Labels are not capped at 5. The free plan allows up to 500, the same as Pro. You can keep a handful of projects for the big areas of your life and use labels like `@errands` or `@calls` for everything else. Label views even have the Board layout on the free plan.

## Three filters

Filters are saved searches, like "everything due this week with priority 1". On the free plan you can keep 3. For many people that is enough: one for work, one for home, one for the week. If you build your whole day around filters, 3 will feel tight fast.

## Reminders

The free plan includes task reminders, which Todoist calls automatic reminders. Custom reminders, the ones you set yourself, are a Pro feature. So are deadlines.

## Calendar

On the free plan you can connect one Google or Outlook calendar account. Your events then show up in Today and Upcoming, and your dated tasks show up in that calendar. You can also subscribe to a Todoist calendar feed on every plan.

What you do not get is the Calendar layout, where you drag tasks into time slots, or task duration. If you time-block your day inside Todoist, you need Pro.

## History, uploads and AI

Activity history on the free plan goes back one week. That matters if you look back at what you finished last month.

Files you attach to comments can be up to 5 MB on the free plan. That covers screenshots and short PDFs, but not much else.

The AI capture features, Ramble and Task Capture Assist, share 10 sessions a month on Beginner. A session counts only when it creates at least one task, and the count resets on the 1st of each month.

## When the free plan is enough

The free plan works well if most of these are true:

- Your tasks fit into 5 areas, with labels doing the rest of the grouping.
- You use Today and Upcoming more than custom filters.
- Automatic reminders are all the reminding you need.
- You plan your day as a list, not as blocks on a calendar.
- You do not need to look further back than a week.

That describes a lot of personal use. Todoist on the free plan is still a very good task list. It has apps for Mac, Windows, Linux, iPhone, iPad and Android, and it reads dates you type in the task name.

## When it is worth paying

Pro is worth $5 a month, or $60 a year, if you run many projects, live inside filters, set your own reminders, or time-block your day in the Calendar layout. Those are the features the free plan holds back.

## When a different app fits better

Sometimes the limits are a sign that you use Todoist differently from how it is built. If you keep pushing against the project cap, a list with no projects at all might suit you better. If you never touch filters, reminders or sharing, you are paying, or planning to pay, for parts you skip.

That is why I built Soonlane, an [open source Todoist alternative](/) that keeps the day-based part of Todoist and drops the rest. It is free with no paid plan, so there are no caps to hit. It has Upcoming, Today, Overdue with a Reschedule button, dates you type like `fri` or `14 Nov`, priorities, repeating tasks and labels. It has no projects, filters, reminders or sharing, so it is not a fit if you need those. [Todoist vs Soonlane](/blog/soonlane-vs-todoist) goes through the trade-offs one by one.
