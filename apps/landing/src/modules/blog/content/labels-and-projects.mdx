Most task apps push every task into a project. You make one for work, one for home, one for a side idea, and every new errand demands a folder. For a team, that fits. A project has an owner, a deadline and an end. A personal list is different. "Call the bank about the mortgage" is about money, about the house, and something to do by phone during office hours. A project makes you pick one box.

In Soonlane, an [open source Todoist alternative](/) organised by days, a task without a project is the normal case. Projects are there when a piece of work needs one home, and labels cut across everything else.

## Projects vs labels: when to use which

A project gives a task at most one home. That helps when the work has a clear boundary, but it gets in the way when you force your whole life into folders.

First, you stop and decide where every small task belongs. When a task fits two folders, you pick one and it goes missing from the other.

Second, a folder tree grows fast. A project for the house gets split into kitchen and garden, and a year later half the lists sit empty in the sidebar.

Third, plain project lists often hide the date. You open "Home" and see twenty items with no sense of what needs doing this week.

A clean split solves all three. Save projects for bigger efforts with a clear scope or finish line, like filing taxes, moving flat, or a client job. If something is just one task with a few steps, use subtasks instead of making a new project. Soonlane lets a task hold up to 50 subtasks, nested up to four levels deep, and completing the parent task completes its open subtasks. Leave everyday errands out of projects altogether.

Then use labels for themes, contexts or people that cut across your list. A task can belong to one project and still carry several labels, so `@phone` or `@money` pulls together matching tasks whether they live in a project or sit loose on a day.

## Label sets that work

Start small. Five to ten labels is plenty. You can add more when you notice a group you keep looking for. Here are sets that work well for a personal list. Pick one, or mix two.

**By area of life.** work, home, health, money, family. Useful if you only create projects for temporary goals and want broad tags for ongoing errands. The mortgage call gets both money and home.

**By where you can do it.** computer, phone, errands, home. Useful when you are out. Open errands and you see everything you can do on the way, across every project.

**By how much it takes.** quick for anything under ten minutes, focus for work that needs a quiet hour. On a tired evening, quick is the list to open.

**By who is involved.** One label per person you often do things for or wait on, or one label called waiting for tasks stuck on someone else's reply.

A task usually needs one or two labels, not a label from every set. If you add five labels to every task, the labels stop telling you anything.

## How labels and projects work in Soonlane

The sidebar has a Projects section above Labels. Projects are free on every plan with no cap, and they have no colors or sections. Each project has a "…" menu with Rename and Delete. Deleting a project keeps its tasks; they only lose the project.

Labels have three entry points when you are working on tasks.

1. In the task card, click the Labels chip. Type in the "Type a label" field to find a label, or pick Create label to make a new one with that name.
2. Type @ in the task name. Soonlane suggests your labels as you type, and can create a new one. "Call the bank @money @phone" saves as "Call the bank" with two labels.
3. Click Add task on a label's page. The new task gets that label and today's date. A project page works the same way: its Add task row adds a task due today into that project.

In the sidebar's Labels section, the + button adds a label, and each label has a "…" menu with Rename, Color and Delete.

Some details worth knowing:

- A label name can be up to 60 characters and can include spaces.
- Names are unique per person, ignoring case. You cannot have both Work and work.
- Color offers Todoist's palette of 20 colors. A label without a color shows in charcoal.
- Deleting a label removes it from every task that had it. The tasks stay.
- Labels are listed alphabetically.

On each task row, the labels show under the name in their colors, next to the due date.

## Views, counts and undated tasks

Next to each label in the sidebar is a number counting its open tasks, so you can see at a glance that errands has 4 waiting and money has 1. A label with no open tasks shows no number.

Click a label and its view opens at `/labels/<name>`. Open tasks without a due date appear first in a "No date" section, followed by dated tasks grouped by day, starting with Overdue and skipping empty days. Project views follow the same order, listing open tasks without a date first and then grouping the rest by day.

Tasks that have neither a due date nor a project go to the Inbox, which shows above Overdue in Upcoming and Today. If you want to reschedule late work, the Overdue section has a Reschedule button that moves all its tasks to one day.

Sorting every view by day keeps dates visible. Open the health label and you see a task to find a new physio under No date, a slipped dentist call under Overdue, a prescription due Thursday, and a blood test in three weeks.

## What projects and labels cannot do

Both features stay simple on purpose. Know the limits before you move a large list over.

There are no filters. You cannot ask for "errands and quick" or "everything except work" in one view. Each project or label page shows one list.

There is no nesting and there are no sections. Projects have no sub-projects or board sections, and labels have no sub-labels. Everything in the sidebar sits at one flat level.

There are no comments or shared projects. Soonlane is built for one person's list.

If you are coming from Todoist, the importer brings your open tasks, projects and labels over with a preview. The post comparing [Soonlane and Todoist](/blog/soonlane-vs-todoist) covers what Soonlane drops and what it keeps.

## A small example

Here is a setup I would hand someone starting out: two projects for active work with an end, such as Tax return and Moving, plus six labels: work, home, money, health, errands, quick. Most daily tasks get no project at all, just a due date and one or two labels where they help.

Use that list for a week and then check the sidebar. If a project has turned into a dumping ground with no end in sight, delete the project and let its tasks live on your days with a label instead. If one label has 30 open tasks and another has none, rename, split or delete until the counts mean something to you.

Labels also reach your calendar. If you subscribe to your tasks in a calendar app, each event's notes list the task's labels. The post on [putting your tasks in Google Calendar and Apple Calendar](/blog/tasks-in-apple-calendar-and-google-calendar) shows how to set that up.
