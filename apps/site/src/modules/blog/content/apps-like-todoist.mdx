Todoist gives you a quick way to add a task, a Today view, due dates you can type in plain words, and repeating tasks. Plenty of apps work the same way, and many of them have a free plan.

I looked at seven that do. I left out apps without a free plan, apps built for team projects, and apps I could not check against their own websites. Prices and limits come from each app's official pages, as of November 2026.

## The short list

| App                | Free plan                   | Paid plan                    | Open source |
| ------------------ | --------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------- |
| TickTick           | 9 lists, 99 tasks per list  | $49.99/year                  | No          |
| Microsoft To Do    | Free app                    | Not listed                   | No          |
| Google Tasks       | Comes with a Google account | Not listed                   | No          |
| Any.do             | Tasks, reminders, calendar  | From $4.99/month             | No          |
| Vikunja            | Free when you host it       | Cloud from 4 euros/month     | AGPL-3.0    |
| Super Productivity | Free, data on your device   | No paid tiers                | MIT         |
| Soonlane           | Free task list and Mac app  | Pro, coming for integrations | AGPL-3.0    |

For reference, Todoist's own free plan allows 5 projects and 3 filters, and Pro costs $5 a month billed yearly. [Todoist free plan limits](/blog/todoist-free-plan-limits) goes through the rest.

## TickTick

TickTick's free plan covers the Todoist basics: lists, a kanban view, dates typed in plain words, task reminders and sync between devices. It gives you 9 lists with up to 99 tasks each and 2 reminders per task. Premium costs $49.99 a year and raises those to 299 lists, 999 tasks per list and 5 reminders per task.

It suits people who want a Todoist-style app with more room on the free plan, as long as 9 lists are enough.

## Microsoft To Do

Microsoft To Do is a free download with apps for Android, iOS, Mac, Windows and the web. Its daily view is called My Day, and you can create lists and share them with other people. It also works inside Outlook, which helps if your email already lives there.

Work and school accounts are different. They need a To Do license and an Exchange Online mailbox from the organisation.

## Google Tasks

Google Tasks comes with every Google account. You open it in a side panel in Gmail, Calendar, Docs and other Google apps, and there are apps for Android and iOS. Tasks can have a date and time, a deadline, subtasks and a repeat. A task with a date shows up in Google Calendar, and Google notifies you at its time.

It is the most basic app here. Tasks live in lists, a task with subtasks cannot repeat, and shared tasks cannot repeat either. It fits if you live in Gmail and Calendar and want tasks next to your events.

## Any.do

Any.do has a free Personal plan with tasks, lists, reminders, a calendar and a daily planner, synced across your devices. Premium costs $4.99 a month billed yearly or $7.99 billed monthly, and lists recurring tasks, location reminders, WhatsApp reminders and colour tags among its extras.

Recurring tasks sit on the Premium side of Any.do's plan comparison. If many of your tasks repeat, check how far the free plan goes before you move.

## Vikunja

Vikunja is open source under AGPL-3.0 and free to run on your own server. It has projects, labels, reminders, repeating tasks, saved filters, and list, kanban, Gantt and table views. You can share a project with other people or with a link. It can import from Todoist, Trello and Microsoft To Do, and it speaks CalDAV, so calendar and task apps can sync with it.

If you do not want to run a server, Vikunja Cloud costs 4 euros a month for the Personal plan, and the team hosts it in the EU.

Vikunja is the best fit here if you want Todoist's projects and filters without the subscription. I cover running it yourself in [self-hosted Todoist alternatives](/blog/self-hosted-todoist-alternative).

## Super Productivity

Super Productivity is open source under the MIT license and free. It runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS and in the browser, and it keeps your tasks on your device without an account. If you want sync, you can use WebDAV, Dropbox or the project's own sync.

It leans towards focused work. It has time tracking, a Pomodoro timer, and it can pull issues from GitHub, GitLab and Jira into your day. It also has projects, tags, subtasks and repeating tasks. Pick it if you track time on tasks or want your data local by default.

## Soonlane

Soonlane is the one I build, so weigh this section with that in mind. It is an [open source Todoist alternative](/) under AGPL-3.0, and it keeps only the day-based part of Todoist.

It opens on Upcoming, one scroll from Overdue through Today and every day after. You type dates like `tom`, `fri` or `14 Nov`, set priorities P1 to P4, and add due times, repeats and labels. A task can have a goal, like 50 push-ups, that you log progress towards. A private calendar link puts your open tasks in Apple Calendar or Google Calendar.

The task list is free, while integrations, starting with the calendar link, will move to a paid Pro plan. The trade-offs are real, though. There are no projects, filters, reminders, subtasks or sharing. Sign-in is Google only. Phones use the web app, and there is no Todoist import.

## One paid option worth knowing

Things is often named next to Todoist, but it has no free plan. The Mac app costs $49.99 on the App Store, with a 15-day trial from the Things website, and the iPhone and iPad apps are sold separately. It runs only on Apple devices.

## Which one to pick

- If you want projects and saved filters without a subscription, try Vikunja.
- If 5 Todoist projects are too few but 9 lists are enough, try TickTick.
- If you live in Outlook or Gmail, try Microsoft To Do or Google Tasks.
- If you want reminders on your phone for free, try Google Tasks, Microsoft To Do or Any.do.
- If you want open source, try Vikunja, Super Productivity or Soonlane.
- If you want a plain list of days with nothing to set up, try Soonlane.

If you are still unsure, sign up for two of them and use each for a week with real tasks.
