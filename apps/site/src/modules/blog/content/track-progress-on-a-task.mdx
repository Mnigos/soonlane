A checkbox is all or nothing. That suits "pay the electricity bill". It does not suit "50 push-ups", which I do in sets of ten through the day, or "read 20 pages", which happens on the train and again before bed. With a plain checkbox, the task sits open all day with no sign that I am 30 push-ups in.

So Soonlane, an [open source Todoist alternative](/), lets a task carry a goal. You set an amount, log progress as you go, and the task completes itself when you reach the amount.

## Tasks that come in amounts

A goal fits any task you finish in pieces and can count. A few that work well:

- 50 push-ups, or 10,000 steps
- Read 20 pages
- 8 glasses of water
- Write 500 words
- Sort 300 photos
- Practise 30 minutes

The amount does not have to be big. "3 job applications" is a good goal too. What matters is that you would otherwise tick the task off only at the end, and you want to see where you are before that.

A goal is not a project plan. If a task breaks into different steps, like "book flights" and "book a hotel", make those separate tasks with a label in common. Goals are for one thing done many times.

## Set a goal

The goal sits in the same card you use to add or edit a task.

1. Add a task, or open an existing one with Edit.
2. Click the Goal chip at the bottom of the card.
3. In Target, enter the amount as a whole number from 1 to 10,000.
4. In Unit, enter what you count, like push-ups or pages. The unit is optional and can be up to 24 characters.
5. Press Enter. The chip now reads "50 push-ups".
6. Save the task.

To take the goal off later, open the task with Edit, click the chip and choose Remove goal.

## Log progress

A task with a goal shows its progress under the name, as "12/50 push-ups", with a thin bar below that fills as you go.

On a laptop, hover over the task or focus it with the keyboard and two buttons appear on the right. On a touch screen they are always visible.

- +1 adds one. Good for glasses of water, or one job application at a time.
- Log amount, the second button, opens a field called Amount. Type a number and press Enter. Good for a set of 10 push-ups or 7 pages.

The Amount field also takes negative numbers, so -10 takes back a set you logged by mistake. Progress never goes below 0 or above the target. If you are at 45 of 50 and log 10, Soonlane counts 5 and the task is done.

Logging updates the list at once, without waiting for the server. Several quick taps on +1 all count. The server adds each one to the stored number, so none get lost.

## When you reach the target

On a task that does not repeat, reaching the target completes it. A "Task completed" message appears with Undo, the same as when you click the circle. Undo takes back the amount that finished it and the task is open again. On Today, a task completed today moves down to Completed.

You can still complete a goal task with the circle. That is useful when you decide 40 push-ups is enough for today. The circle completes the task and leaves the logged progress as it was.

Editing the target can finish a task too. If you have logged 30 and lower the target to 30, the edit completes the task, with the same message and Undo. A higher target just leaves more to go.

## Goals on a repeating task

Goals are most useful on repeating tasks. A daily "50 push-ups" or a weekday "Read 20 pages" is a habit, and the goal belongs to each day's occurrence.

When you reach the target on a repeating task, it moves to its next date and the progress starts again at 0. Tomorrow's push-ups begin at 0/50, the same as any new day. Completing a repeating goal task with the circle does the same: it moves to the next date and resets the progress.

If a day ends before you reach the target, the task stays open with its progress. It shows in Overdue the next day as "35/50 push-ups", so you can finish the last 15, move it with Reschedule, or complete it with the circle to move on. You can read how next dates are worked out in the post on [repeating tasks](/blog/repeating-tasks), and what to do with a pile of slipped ones in [what to do with overdue tasks](/blog/what-to-do-with-overdue-tasks).

Undo works here as well. After a log or a click on the circle completes the occurrence, Undo puts the task back on its previous date with the progress it had.

One more detail: if the task has already moved to its next date in another tab or on another device, a log from an older screen counts toward the current date and does not move it again. You will not skip a day by tapping +1 twice in two windows.

## What Soonlane does not track

A goal holds one number, the progress of the current occurrence. When a repeating task moves on, that number starts at 0. Soonlane does not keep a history of past days and does not draw charts.

There are no streaks, scores or reminders either. I left those out on purpose. A goal is there to answer "how much is left today", not to grade the week.

If you want a log of every workout, a fitness app will do that better. If you want the push-ups to sit in the same list as the rest of your day, with the count right under the name, a goal is enough.

## A quick setup to try

Pick one habit you already count. Add it as a task due today, set it to repeat every day, and give it a goal with a unit. Log the first set now. Tomorrow it will be waiting at 0, on the next day in Upcoming.
